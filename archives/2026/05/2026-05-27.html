AI Latest 01 Figma Learn

Rolling out from March 26, 2026

Figma Make kits give AI prototypes a real design-system spine

Figma is quietly solving one of the messiest problems in AI-assisted interface work: the gap between a clever prompt and a prototype that actually respects the system. Make kits let teams package npm assets, published variables, library styles, and written guidelines into a reusable starting point so generated work begins inside the right rails instead of improvising around them.

For PMI’s Graphic Design Team, the bigger signal is operational. This turns design-system judgment into something teams can publish, approve, and reuse across prompts. The interesting shift is not “AI makes screens faster,” but “AI can now inherit more of the decisions that normally live in a senior designer’s head or a buried setup doc.”

AI Latest 02 Figma Learn

Current help documentation

Figma’s attachment workflow makes prompt grounding much more literal

The most useful part of Figma’s attachment guidance is how blunt it is. Teams can attach images, design files, and PDFs, then tell Make whether they want a 1:1 reconstruction, a looser style reference, or direct asset embedding. That sounds small, but it changes the conversation from vague inspiration prompts to explicit art direction.

This matters in brand-sensitive studio work because PDFs can include guidelines, design specs, or presentation rules, while images can be embedded or used as reference. It is a reminder that the quality jump in generative design is increasingly coming from better context packaging, not just stronger models.

Canva’s API update turns layout fixes and brand checks into programmable actions

Canva’s new Design Editing API is one of the more concrete workflow stories in the current AI wave. It suggests a near future where repetitive cleanup, adaptation, and governance tasks happen through connected apps inside the design surface itself. For a team juggling decks, social variants, and campaign resizes, that is the kind of change that actually saves cognitive load.

AI Latest 07 Microsoft Learn

Release notes updated last month

PowerPoint Copilot is finally exposing stronger art-direction controls

Microsoft’s release notes show a meaningful deck-making shift: Copilot now lets users steer presentation length, narrative tone, slide style, and AI-generated images when building presentations. That is a stronger creative-control story than the old “make me a deck” framing, because it acknowledges that structure and visual tone are not afterthoughts.

This does not turn PowerPoint into a design tool in the Figma sense, but it does make it more realistic for fast internal storytelling. For teams producing executive summaries, training decks, and campaign readouts, those controls mean less rewriting after the first draft and a better chance of landing within the right voice from the outset.

PowerPoint’s model switcher makes deck AI a taste choice, not just a default

Microsoft is now documenting model choice directly inside PowerPoint editing flows, including Auto, ChatGPT, and Claude. AI Latest 09 OpenAI Academy

April 10, 2026

OpenAI’s image-generation guide is starting to sound like a design handbook

OpenAI’s image-generation guidance is most interesting when it drops the magic talk and starts sounding like production advice. The useful bits are very concrete: ask for sharp text rendering on dense layouts, use reference photos when likeness matters, and prefer “generic” or ownable design directions instead of imitation-heavy prompts.

That is the kind of practical language design teams can actually operationalize. It also reinforces a healthier workflow expectation: generative images can accelerate concepting and comp creation, but dense information design and polished typography still benefit from finishing in dedicated layout tools.
