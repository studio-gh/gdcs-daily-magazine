Anthropic • Design Workflow

Claude Design is less a chatbot stunt than a bid to become a real first-draft surface for decks, prototypes, and one-pagers.

Anthropic’s design product matters because it is explicitly aiming at creative work products, not only ideation. The positioning around slides, presentations, mockups, and export to formats like PPTX and Canva makes it much more relevant to studio teams than generic agent news.

For PMI, the important shift is where the first draft gets born. If more rough visual work starts inside conversational tools, the design team’s leverage moves toward direction, refinement, and system control rather than being the sole origin point of every frame.

Figma • Review Workflow

Commenting in Figma Make turns live prototypes into reviewable design objects, not just generated experiments.

This rollout is more meaningful than it looks. Figma is adapting one of its core collaboration habits, comments, to an AI-generated environment where the output is interactive, versioned, and constantly in flux.

The design implication is strong: feedback stays anchored to visible states, review history becomes easier to preserve, and AI prototyping starts behaving more like a shareable team artifact. That makes Make less isolated from established design practice and much more usable inside studio critique cycles.

Figma • Publishing

Figma Make is quietly becoming a publishing surface, with metadata, access control, and share-image settings that feel closer to product launch than mockup handoff.

The publishing controls around Figma Make deserve attention because they push AI-made work past internal experiment status. Titles, meta descriptions, internal-only access, password protection, favicons, social sharing images, and custom domains all signal a more mature output layer.

For Creative Studio, that changes the threshold for what can be prototyped and circulated early. Designers can now think about shareability, discoverability, and editorial framing much earlier in the process, instead of treating those as downstream finishing tasks.

Microsoft Copilot • Brand Systems

Official brand kits inside Copilot make on-brand PowerPoint and visual generation feel more like infrastructure than a nice extra.

This feature matters because it brings governance right into the generation layer. Microsoft is not just giving people a place to store logos and colors; it is building a framework that can shape posters, banners, images, and presentations by default.

That is highly relevant to PMI’s design team. As more presentation and communications work gets drafted by non-designers, brand kits become one of the clearest ways to protect hierarchy, voice, picture treatment, and layout behavior without slowing everything down.

Google’s Workspace icon refresh is one of today’s clearest reminders that system clarity still matters in an AI-saturated tool stack.

Not every important design story is a feature drop. As generative tools make products busier and blur the boundaries between apps, clear iconography becomes more valuable because it helps users orient themselves faster and trust the system around them.

For Creative Studio, this is a useful watch item precisely because it is small. When product families evolve quickly, visual identity work has to carry more usability weight, not less. That is a lesson worth borrowing for internal ecosystems and slide-library systems too.

Google Workspace • Google Pics

Google is pushing image generation and editing directly toward Workspace, starting with the kinds of visual tasks that usually spill into Slides.

The newest Workspace update matters because Google Pics is not being framed as a standalone toy. Google is explicitly integrating image generation and editing into Workspace apps, beginning with Slides and Drive, which puts visual work much closer to the day-to-day presentation flow.

That means more image drafting will happen inside productivity software before design ever sees it. The design response should not be panic; it should be stronger standards for what gets elevated, what needs polish, and where brand-quality visuals still need expert intervention.

Google Vids • Motion Workflow

Google Vids is making motion content cheaper to try, which could change who shows up to review with video instead of slides.

Google Vids is becoming more relevant to creative teams because the barrier to trying motion is getting lower. Free generations, custom music, directable avatars, and browser capture make it easier for non-specialists to bring moving assets into everyday communications.

That raises a design question PMI’s team should care about: when do fast AI video drafts improve a story, and when do they simply add movement without meaning? As more stakeholders can make motion, the bar for editorial judgment becomes more important, not less.

Canva’s latest release bundle is interesting because it mixes AI with the less glamorous workflow fixes teams actually feel every day.

That blend matters for designers because real software adoption rarely turns on one hero feature. It turns on whether the rough edges around editing, presenting, and distributing work keep getting removed while the AI layer becomes more useful.
