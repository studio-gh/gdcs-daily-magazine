Campaigns to Watch

Current campaigns worth studying for strategy, execution, audience insight, risk, timing, and measurable project leadership. Not ads to admire from a distance - project systems to learn from.

Editor’s pick
Nike / FIFA World Cup 2026

Nike builds a football universe before the tournament begins

Why it matters: Campaign Live reports that Nike spent weeks “dropping breadcrumbs” and building an expansive football universe around its 2026 World Cup campaign, with collaborations across soccer, entertainment, fashion, and lifestyle.

What to watch: The upside is anticipation by design; the risk is complexity. A campaign universe needs sequencing, governance, partner alignment, and a clear release rhythm so the audience can follow the story.

Project leadership takeaway: Big launches are not single moments. Treat them as portfolio programs: map dependencies, phase the reveals, and make every partner touchpoint ladder back to one strategic idea.

Source: Campaign Live (trade source; access may vary)

Global portfolio
DoorDash / Deliveroo / Wolt

DoorDash unites three brands around World Cup anticipation

Why it matters: Campaign Live’s sports brief says DoorDash launched “Deliver Us To Fútbol,” its first global campaign celebrating FIFA World Cup 2026 with Deliveroo and Wolt as part of the same official on-demand ecosystem.

What to watch: Global consistency is useful only if local teams can still execute with cultural fluency. Watch how the campaign balances shared assets with market-level rituals, languages, and fan behaviors.

Project leadership takeaway: Portfolio alignment is a leadership skill. Define the shared operating model early, then give regional teams clear decision rights so execution can move quickly without fragmenting the brand.

Source: Campaign Live (trade source; access may vary)

Audience listening
Dove / Coachella

Dove turns a viral audience complaint into a sponsorship idea

Why it matters: Campaign Live reports that a TikTok about festival deodorant needs reached 23 million viewers and 3.2 million likes, after which Dove became the official deodorant sponsor of Coachella’s host city, Indio, for 2026.

What to watch: The insight is strong because it came from audience behavior, not a boardroom hunch. The risk is tone: brands must solve the problem without making people feel watched or mocked.

Project leadership takeaway: Build listening loops into delivery. When teams can convert real audience friction into timely action, they create value with both creativity and operational speed.

Source: Campaign Live (trade source; access may vary)

Experience design
Gov Ball activations

Newcomer brands compete for attention at Governors Ball

Why it matters: Campaign Live reports that Governors Ball hosted more than 30 brand activations in 2026, including a Listerine swish tour and Tinder arcade. The useful lesson is not spectacle; it is context fit.

What to watch: Festivals can reward novelty but punish weak operations. Measurement should go beyond foot traffic to recall, opt-ins, sampling quality, earned reach, and whether the experience actually matched the audience moment.

Project leadership takeaway: Experiential work is a live project. Scenario-plan the queue, weather, staffing, safety, brand handoff, and post-event measurement before the first visitor arrives.

Source: Campaign Live (trade source; access may vary)

Inspiration

Signals, standards, and prompts to help project professionals lead change with confidence, imagination, and evidence. PMI sources first; external references only when they add a useful project lesson.

Governance signal
PMI AI Standard

AI project work now has a stronger governance anchor

Why it resonates: PMI announced the world’s first global standard for AI in project work, describing it as a principle-driven, technology-agnostic framework for professionals delivering AI-related initiatives.

What project professionals can take from it: AI adoption needs more than experimentation. It needs accountable decision-making, shared language, risk awareness, and governance that can travel across portfolios.

Question for the team: Where are we already using AI faster than our governance, training, or stakeholder alignment can support?

Source: PMI

Capability shift
PMP exam update

The profession is making AI, sustainability, and value delivery visible

Why it resonates: PMI says the PMP exam will be updated in July 2026 to reflect the future of project management, including new focus areas such as AI, sustainability, and value delivery.

What project professionals can take from it: The signal is clear: project success is increasingly measured by adaptive leadership, business value, responsible delivery, and the ability to work with emerging technology.

Question for the team: Which skill area would most improve our next project: AI fluency, sustainability thinking, or value measurement?

Source: PMI

Upskilling gap
AI confidence

AI confidence is becoming a leadership responsibility

Why it resonates: PMI thought leadership says unpublished PMI Customer Experience research found only about 20% of project managers report extensive or good practical experience with AI tools and technologies, while 49% report little to no experience or understanding in a project-management context.

What project professionals can take from it: Confidence will not come from a single tool demo. It comes from safe practice, ethics, use-case clarity, and managers who help teams learn without shame.

Question for the team: What is one low-risk workflow where we can practice using AI, document what changes, and decide what should become standard?

Source: PMI
