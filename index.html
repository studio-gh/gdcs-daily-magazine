          <h2>Claude Design turns the AI brief into a working design surface</h2>

          <p>Why it matters for PMI’s design team: Anthropic is moving Claude closer to a real concepting studio, with a new design environment that can assemble artifacts, interpret brand inputs, and export work into formats the team already uses. For Creative Studio, that changes AI from a research sidecar into a serious early-phase option for campaign layouts, event one-pagers, and fast internal visualization.</p>

          <p>The practical implication is not final-production replacement; it is speed at the fuzzy front of the process. Claude Design looks strongest when the team needs multiple directions before opening heavier production tools, especially for workshops, stakeholder alignment, or fast iteration on narrative structure before brand refinement in Figma, Adobe, or PowerPoint.</p>

          <p>Internally, this is a workflow discussion as much as a tooling discussion. PMI should define how AI-generated first drafts enter review, what brand materials are safe to expose, and where human creative judgment must remain non-negotiable in the shift from concept to polished asset.</p>

          <h2>Claude for Creative Work points toward a real creative-ops layer</h2>

          <p>Why it matters for PMI’s design team: this is Anthropic’s clearest signal yet that AI value for creatives will come from cross-tool coordination, not just better prompting. The more Claude can connect into asset libraries, production apps, and approval materials, the more it becomes useful for prep work, repetitive tasks, and internal handoff friction that normally drains studio time.</p>
