This Week's Edition

The Editable AI Stack

This week, the creative software story is not just generation. It is editability, context, brand control, and the return of the canvas as the place where teams decide what is worth making.

07.01

Editorial thesis: AI is moving deeper into the actual surfaces of creative work: slides, canvases, timelines, type systems, image layers, and brand libraries. The winning updates this week do not promise effortless magic; they promise drafts that can be questioned, edited, governed, and pushed further by people with taste.

Publishing note: Built for the July 1 weekly edition, with 10 core stories followed by Campaigns to Watch and Inspiration for Creative Studio. Best published with AI and design workflow news leading the issue.

Core Stories

AI, design tools, presentation workflows, and creative production

Gemini in Slides now builds editable presentations from one prompt

Google Slides / Presentation AI / Workflow

Google's June 30 update gives Gemini in Slides a practical new job: generate a full multi-slide deck, ground it in Drive files, match another presentation's style, and leave the result as editable native slides. For design teams, the interesting part is not the prompt; it is the style-reference loop. Presentation AI is getting closer to the real production brief: use our context, respect our look, and give us something we can still art-direct.

Source

Figma turns the canvas into a place for code, motion, shaders, and agents

Figma Config / Canvas / Expressive Systems

Config 2026 was unusually clear about Figma's ambition: the canvas is no longer just a layout surface. It is becoming a live workshop for code layers, motion, shader effects, generative plugins, Weave tools, and AI agents. The editorial angle for Creative Studio is that Figma is reframing code as a design material, not a handoff destination. That changes where experimentation happens and who gets to participate.

Source

Figma's agent gets more context, custom tools, and real team memory

Figma Agent / Plugins / Team Workflows

Figma's design agent is moving beyond one-off prompting toward context-aware collaboration. The June 24 update emphasizes custom tools, skills, playground files, and agent actions that can generate plugins, code layers, shaders, and accessibility checks directly on the canvas. This is a useful distinction for designers: a generic assistant produces; a team-aware agent can start to understand how a studio actually works.

Source

Adobe brings its creative agent into Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, Premiere, and Frame.io

Adobe Creative Cloud / Agentic Production / Craft

Adobe's most important June move is less about a single flashy output and more about orchestration. AI Assistant is now in public beta across Premiere, Photoshop, Illustrator, Frame.io, and InDesign, with examples that include batch background removal, layer organization, versioned Illustrator files from spreadsheet data, print-readiness checks, and surfacing feedback across revisions. Adobe is making the case that agentic AI belongs in production plumbing, where creative teams lose hours.

Source

Adobe's Creative Cloud updates target the tedious middle of making

Adobe / Lightroom / Premiere / Illustrator

The June 15 Creative Cloud update is full of small but consequential friction removals: Assisted Culling in Lightroom, Photo to Video powered by Firefly and Google Veo, object masking and single-word captioning in Premiere, object matte tools in After Effects, reflection removal in Photoshop, and Concept to Vector in Illustrator. The through-line is control. Adobe is betting designers will accept AI when it turns rough or repetitive work into editable material rather than finished-looking mush.

Source

PowerPoint adds stronger AI image generation and broader brand-asset access

Microsoft 365 Copilot / PowerPoint / Brand Systems

Microsoft's June 16 Copilot release notes add Black Forest Labs' FLUX.2 Flex model inside PowerPoint, with improved text and layout rendering for generated visuals. Just as important for enterprise creative teams: Copilot for PowerPoint can now draw from multiple SharePoint Organizational Asset Libraries when creating presentations. That makes the AI deck workflow more brand-real, because approved imagery and visual governance are part of the system instead of an afterthought.

Source

NotebookLM becomes a deeper research-to-output studio

Google NotebookLM / Research / Editorial Production

Google's June 8 NotebookLM upgrade gives the research tool more advanced reasoning, code execution, source discovery, and the ability to generate reports, charts, spreadsheets, and slide decks. For editorial and design teams, this is worth watching because research synthesis is becoming a production surface. The tool is not just summarizing source material; it is starting to help package arguments, evidence, and visual outputs from the same notebook.

Source

OpenAI positions Codex for creative production and product design workflows

OpenAI Codex / Creative Plugins / Prototyping

OpenAI's June 2 Codex update expands the product beyond software development with role-specific plugins. The creative production plugin is aimed at turning briefs into reviewable assets: campaign boards, display ad variations, product lifestyle shots, and ecommerce-ready image sets using tools including Figma, Canva, Shutterstock, Picsart, and Fal. The product design plugin focuses on early prototypes, live URL audits, and interactive screenshots. The signal is clear: AI workspaces want to sit upstream of design tools, not beside them.

Source

Canva and Claude make AI artifacts editable instead of disposable

Canva / Claude Design / HTML Editing

Canva's integration with Claude Design is an important bridge between generative output and everyday design editing. Claude can draft visual work, then Canva turns it into fully editable, structured designs. Canva is also importing AI-generated HTML into its drag-and-drop editor, so landing pages, widgets, and interactive artifacts can be moved, recolored, updated, embedded, or published without regenerating the whole thing. That is the key shift: AI artifacts are becoming working files.

Source

Figma's AI report says the real change is multiplayer, not personal productivity

Figma Research / Collaboration / Design Strategy

Figma's 2026 AI report gives the week's product announcements a useful frame. Based on 8,403 survey responses and 639 interviews, the report says AI is now changing team collaboration more than individual output. Designers are participating more in development, developers are doing more design work, and 76% of product builders say at least half their work happens on the canvas. The takeaway for Creative Studio: AI raises the importance of taste, shared spaces, and decision-making, not just speed.

Campaigns to Watch

The strongest campaign references this week use cultural specificity and brand-native systems rather than generic seasonal participation.

IKEA Canada / Dentsu Creative

Assemble the World turns product recognition into a World Cup guessing game

Why it matters: IKEA Canada uses everyday objects as national-flag components for the 2026 World Cup, turning familiar product forms into playful visual puzzles. It is social-first, recognizably IKEA, and clever without abandoning the brand's retail vocabulary.

Creative Studio takeaway: This is a useful model for campaign systems built from owned assets. The product catalog becomes the design kit.

Source

Levi's / Pride 2026

Levi's Pride work leans into queer biker history, not rainbow surface treatment

Why it matters: Creative Bloq's Pride roundup highlights Levi's 2026 Pride collection for drawing from queer biker clubs and leather culture, while continuing support for LGBTQ+ advocacy groups. The lesson is specificity: subculture, material history, and action read stronger than seasonal decoration.

Creative Studio takeaway: When designing around identity or community, start with a real visual archive and a real support mechanism.

Source

Adweek / Cannes Lions 2026

Cannes contenders show the craft counterweight to AI spectacle

Why it matters: Adweek's Cannes forecast argues that the most admired work, even in a year shaped by AI, is winning attention through human feeling, storytelling, and craft rather than technological novelty. That is a useful corrective for every AI-heavy production conversation.

Creative Studio takeaway: Use AI to accelerate variants and production, but keep the winning idea rooted in emotion, behavior, and cultural tension.

Inspiration

Bookmark these visual directions for Creative Studio: expressive type, editable systems, tactile resistance to AI gloss, and motion as a brand behavior.

Typography

June type releases favor range: workhorse monospace, luxury stencil, and heavy display systems

Creative Boom's June type roundup is especially useful for editorial and brand work because it spans practical data-facing families and display faces with serious poster presence. Watch the contrast between strict constructed type and softer, high-contrast serif experiments.

Visual Trend

Anti-AI craft is becoming a mainstream design response

The broad 2026 trend conversation keeps returning to texture, collage, imperfection, expressive type, and warm human marks. Treat this less as nostalgia and more as a balancing system: precision from AI, evidence of touch from design direction.

Motion Systems

Motion is now part of identity, not decoration after the fact

As Figma Motion and shader tools land on the same canvas as layout and code, brand systems should define behavior as carefully as color and type. Glow, blink, pulse, reveal, drag, and transition styles are becoming recognizable brand assets.
