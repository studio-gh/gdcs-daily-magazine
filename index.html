Section Three

Inspiration

Three craft prompts for translating the week's tool news into stronger editorial and presentation work.

Use AI speed to protect human texture.

The through-line this week is editable, structured output: layers, brand systems, reusable sources, and tool connectors.

Use that saved production time to make one deliberate human choice: a sharper crop, a more tactile type treatment, or motion that clarifies structure.

Typography

Use bold monospace labels against generous sans body copy for clean, technical hierarchy.

Layout

Give each spread one dominant visual decision: crop, rhythm, frame, edge, or full-width media.

Motion

For slides, reveal source first, then structure, then the final editorial takeaway.

Friday Scan

Top stories of the week

A quick readout of the week's biggest creative implications, plus trend reports to keep nearby.

1. Editable AI output is the week's clearest theme.

Canva, Claude, Figma, and OpenAI are all moving away from disposable generations and toward layered, revisable, system-aware work.

2. The design-to-code gap is getting more visual.

Figma Make's local-code update makes the handoff loop feel less like translation and more like shared inspection.

3. Presentation AI is becoming canvas-native.

Microsoft's Copilot redesign and NotebookLM source syncing both point to AI living closer to the deck, brief, and source file.

4. Brand systems are becoming machine-readable.

Custom models, Brand System in Affinity, and Claude Design all suggest the next advantage is governed style, not sheer generation volume.
