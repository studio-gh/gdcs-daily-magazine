01 • Anthropic • 17 Apr 2026 Exploration to prototype

Claude Design turns the AI brief into a working design surface

Why it matters for PMI’s design team: Anthropic is moving Claude closer to a real concepting studio, with a new design environment that can assemble artifacts, interpret brand inputs, and export work into formats the team already uses. For Creative Studio, that changes AI from a research sidecar into a serious early-phase option for campaign layouts, event one-pagers, and fast internal visualization.

The practical implication is not final-production replacement; it is speed at the fuzzy front of the process. Claude Design looks strongest when the team needs multiple directions before opening heavier production tools, especially for workshops, stakeholder alignment, or fast iteration on narrative structure before brand refinement in Figma, Adobe, or PowerPoint.

Internally, this is a workflow discussion as much as a tooling discussion. PMI should define how AI-generated first drafts enter review, what brand materials are safe to expose, and where human creative judgment must remain non-negotiable in the shift from concept to polished asset.

ChatGPT Images 2.0 makes AI imagery more useful for real design comps

Why it matters for PMI’s design team: OpenAI is pushing image generation toward the practical details designers actually care about, especially typography, layout fidelity, multilingual rendering, and more deliberate control over composition. 04 • OpenAI • 22 Apr 2026 Team workflow systems

Workspace agents in ChatGPT shift AI from solo prompting to team process

Why it matters for PMI’s design team: shared agents make ChatGPT more relevant to studio operations, not just individual experimentation. For a creative team that juggles campaigns, presentation requests, chapter support, and internal stakeholders, a repeatable AI layer for briefs, summaries, reference gathering, and status synthesis can create leverage even when the output is not visual.

The most practical near-term use cases are operational: kickoff brief assembly, weekly inspiration curation, stakeholder summary drafts, content-source digestion, and pre-review packets for presentation or campaign work. What matters internally is governance. PMI should test which agents are worth standardizing, what sources they can safely access, and where escalation to a human editor must happen so convenience never outruns trust.

07 • Canva • 11 Mar 2026 Layer recovery

Magic Layers tackles one of creative production’s most annoying problems

Why it matters for PMI’s design team: flattened visuals are where too much production time disappears, whether the source is a legacy asset, a vendor export, an AI concept, or a fast-turn marketing file that never came with proper layers. Canva’s Magic Layers is directly aimed at recovering editability, which makes it unusually practical compared with more attention-grabbing AI features.

For PMI, the most immediate use case is asset salvage. External partner materials, older campaign graphics, chapter-provided visuals, and concept art can all create rework when they arrive as images instead of structured files. If layer recovery is reliable enough, it reduces rebuild work and speeds adaptation into current templates.

This is a good candidate for a focused internal experiment: pick a small set of archived PMI assets and third-party visuals, then compare recovery quality against manual recreation time. If the lift is meaningful, Magic Layers could become a quiet but important productivity win.
