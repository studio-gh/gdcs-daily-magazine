Editor’s letter 01

Polish is cheap.
Revision is power.

This week’s most useful releases share a stubborn idea: creative work should remain alive after the prompt. Adobe is carrying editable production into Gemini and Claude. Canva is recovering layers from generated images. Figma is turning motion and generative frames into governed, reusable infrastructure.

The pattern extends beyond software. Stone Island’s collectors keep their own voices inside a strict visual grammar. HSBC scales global photography without making every market look interchangeable. Even the strongest packaging work treats the vessel, label and product as one revisable system.

For studios, the question is no longer whether an image looks finished. Ask what remains open one minute later: Can the team adjust it, localize it, trace the decision, and hand it to somebody else without starting over?

— The Creative Intelligence Desk

The editable system

Generation gets useful when the working file survives.

Three platform shifts redraw the boundary between a clever demo and accountable studio production.

01 / Adobe

The chatbot becomes a production surface.

Photoshop, Lightroom, Express and Firefly now run through Gemini, while Claude gains Acrobat, a layer-based Express editor and more than 80 Adobe tools. The headline is not the assistant. It is the route back into editable layers, pages and channel variants.

“The premium feature is not magic. It is recoverable structure.”

Steal this move 05

HSBC / We’re There

Split global production into three lanes.

01

Hero

Commission the images that must set the master tone.

02

Local author

Give city-specific photographers room to make culturally precise work.

03

Licensed

Fill the system with existing work that already has lived-in credibility.

HSBC produced 70 stills across 10 cities using hero shoots, local commissions and licensed imagery from a 14-photographer network. The structure balances control, specificity and cost without forcing every market through one camera.

Try this: Label every planned asset hero, local-author or licensed before the estimate is approved. Then write one non-negotiable and one freedom for each lane.

Debate item 06

PENNY / Comment Coupons

When the audience writes the good line, who owns the creative value?

For

Pay the co-creator.

Rewarding entertaining comments acknowledges that audience language is cultural production, not engagement residue. Coupons and outdoor placements make the exchange visible.

Against

Do not automate taste.

AI scoring can turn vernacular wit into a promotion mechanic, with opaque judgments and pressure to perform for a brand. Moderation and attribution become part of the creative system.
