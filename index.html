AI Latest

The main event today is not another broad model race. It is how AI is moving into the surfaces where designers already build: prompts becoming slides, research becoming visual systems, 3D models entering asset pipelines, and assistant buttons showing up inside the work canvas.

AI Latest 01Google · visual systems

Google’s I/O notebook turns AI into a visual-identity collaborator

Google’s I/O 2026 recap is most useful to designers when read as a studio-process story: the team used Gemini to help generate visual identity directions, expand variations, and test how an event system could stretch across surfaces without losing its core rhythm.

The takeaway is not that AI made the brand. It is that large-scale identity work is becoming more iterative at the edges, where designers need many coherent variations for motion, signage, social, keynotes, and environmental graphics. That is a practical shift for teams managing global systems like PMI’s.

AI Latest 03Figma · idea to product

Figma frames AI as a bridge between concept, prototype, and product

Figma’s latest AI workflow writing is valuable because it does not treat generation as a single artifact moment. It shows the loop: explore the idea, use the design system, prototype faster, and keep the work close enough to real implementation that feedback stays concrete.

For a graphic design team, the parallel is campaign production. The more AI can stay connected to components, content rules, and reusable systems, the less it behaves like novelty output and the more it becomes a way to pressure-test options before craft refinement.

Microsoft’s dynamic Copilot action button is a small interface change with workflow implications. Instead of asking people to leave Word, Excel, or PowerPoint to remember what AI can do, Microsoft is putting contextual prompts directly near the work surface.
