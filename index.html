Campaigns to Watch

Three campaign signals from the week worth studying for system design, cultural timing, and the way brands package participation.

Editor’s pick
Adidas / Oasis

“Original Forever” makes nostalgia operational

Why it matters: The campaign works because the Oasis reunion is not treated as a borrowed reference. Adidas turns cultural timing into a product-and-story system with clear emotional ownership.

What to watch: The balance between memory and merch. Nostalgia can flatten fast unless the campaign gives fans something specific to recognize, wear, share, and argue about.

Source: ADWEEK

Brand system
The Ordinary

The Ordinary keeps proving that restraint can be loud

Why it matters: The brand’s Cannes recognition is a reminder that distinctive systems are not always maximal. A disciplined voice, recognizable visual behavior, and category tension can carry the work.

What to watch: How the brand keeps translating scientific plainness into cultural presence without becoming generic wellness minimalism.

Source: ADWEEK

Gaming behavior
Clash Royale

“Copycats Welcome” turns imitation into participation

Why it matters: Clash Royale’s Cannes recognition shows a smart inversion: instead of simply defending against copycats, the campaign treats imitation as a behavior that can reactivate attention.

What to watch: The idea is strongest when it stays playful and self-aware. The lesson for creative teams is to look for the audience behavior hiding inside the annoyance.

Source: ADWEEK

Inspiration

Visual and workflow prompts for Creative Studio: not just what happened, but what to notice when these tools start shaping taste, files, and decks.

Visual pattern
Claude Design

Audit the “AI-designed” look before it audits you

Why it resonates: Warm neutrals, serif headlines, tracked labels, soft UI primitives, and familiar component libraries can now become a default aesthetic at machine speed.

Studio prompt: Build a small “avoid list” for AI-generated layout work: default palettes, stock component rhythms, generic type pairings, and over-polished landing-page symmetry.

Source: The New Yorker

Canvas prompt
Figma Config

Treat motion, code, and agents as one design review

Why it resonates: Figma’s new surface suggests design review can no longer stop at static frames. Motion logic, coded behavior, shader texture, and agent-generated variants need critique too.

Studio prompt: Add one review pass called “behavior and automation”: what moves, what is generated, what is editable, and what the system might repeat poorly.

Source: Figma

Research prompt
NotebookLM

Let the research agent draft; make the editor decide

Why it resonates: NotebookLM can reduce the blank-page drag between source gathering and presentation structure, but the work still needs hierarchy, skepticism, and visual pacing.

Studio prompt: For every AI-generated deck outline, ask: what is sourced, what is inferred, what is missing, and where does the audience need a visual proof point?

Source: Google
